Special Notices & Amendments

Notices Amendment 6 to the NASA ARMD Research Opportunities in Aeronautics (ROA) 2019 NRA has been posted on the NSPIRES site. NASA&rsquo;s System-Wide Safety (SWS) project has two primary goals. The first is to study and understand how safety can be affected by the growing complexity of advanced aviation systems designed to improve the efficiency of flight, and the increasing number and type of aircraft &ndash; piloted and otherwise &ndash; available to an ever-growing number and type of customers. The second is to develop and demonstrate the research tools, innovative technologies and operational methods that will proactively mitigate those potential risks in order to maintain the aviation industry&rsquo;s unparalleled safety record. SWS activities are designed to enable a system that provides expanded safety awareness, increased access to relevant data, integrated analysis capabilities, improved real-time detection and alerting of hazards, full decision support, and automated safety mitigation strategies where appropriate. The object of this solicitation is to seek proposals in three subtopic areas that complement and build upon NASA&rsquo;s internal research, and also related developments by others in industry, government, and academia. These subtopics include a demonstration of an integrated domain-specific, In-time Aviation Safety Management System (IASMS), the development of novel and predictive on-line and automated analytics-based risk assessment tools, and also the development of trusted contingency management methods. This NRA invites proposers to submit their proposals by September 21, 2020 5:00 PM EST. All proposals must be submitted electronically through NSPIRES at https://nspires.nasaprs.com **UPDATE** Proposal Due Date Extension: The due date for proposal submissions in response to the TTT opportunity has been extended to June 5, 2020. Amendment 2 to the NASA ARMD Research Opportunities in Aeronautics (ROA) 2019 NRA has been posted on the NSPIRES site. Research proposals are sought in seven subtopic areas for Appendix D.2 in support of the Transformational Tools and Technologies (TTT) Project. TTT advances state-of-the-art computational and experimental tools and technologies that are vital to aviation applications in six strategic thrusts. TTT develops computer-based tools, computational fluid dynamics models, and associated scientific knowledge to analyze, understand, and predict aviation concept performance. TTT explores technologies to advance ARMD strategic outcomes including understanding new types of strong and lightweight materials, innovative controls techniques, and experimental methods. The TTT Materials and Structures Discipline emphasizes improved multifunctional and high temperature materials for airframe and engine application, and integrated multiscale modeling and simulation tool development to improve validated first-principles materials and structural modeling. The investment in models and cyber-physical-social ecosystem framework supports the recently developed 2040 Vision study. TTT develops multifunctional materials and adaptive structures for lightweight actuation and mechanical components (e.g., tribological components, energy storage, and thermal management) for reconfigurable aircraft component concepts (airframe and engine). Development of multifunctional materials and structural configurations can reduce weight and enable innovative aircraft component concepts while simultaneously meeting multiple airframe or engine performance requirements. TTT develops improved Multi-Disciplinary Design, Analysis, &amp; Optimization (MDAO) and systems analysis tools to enable multi-disciplinary integration. These technologies support and enable concept development and benefits assessment across ARMD programs and disciplines. NOIs are due April 2, 2020 and Proposals are due April 27, 2020. Amendment 1 to the NASA ARMD Research Opportunities in Aeronautics (ROA) 2019 NRA has been posted on the NSPIRES site. University Student Research Challenge (solicitation NNH19ZEA001N-USRC) seeks to challenge students to propose new aeronautics ideas/concepts that are relevant to NASA Aeronautics. USRC will provide students, from accredited U.S. colleges or universities, with grants for their projects and it includes the challenge of raising a modest amount of cost share funds through crowdfunding platform. The process of creating and preparing a crowdfunding campaign acts as a teaching accelerator - requiring students to act like entrepreneurs and taking action. Crowdfunding also raises awareness about students&rsquo; research among the public. The solicitation goal can be accomplished through project ideas such as advancing the design, developing technology or capabilities in support of aviation, by demonstrating a novel concept, or enabling advancement of aeronautics-related technologies. There have been a number of changes from the previous USRC pilot project, including NASA providing a larger share of funds and half of that being provided upfront. Notices of Intent (NOIs) are not required for this solicitation. Proposals can be submitted at any time and will be evaluated in three cycles: October 30, 2019, February 26, 2020, and June 24, 2020.
